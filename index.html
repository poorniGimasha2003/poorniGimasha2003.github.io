Current research

Structural pruning of neural networks

Individual research project, Oct 2026 – present. I'm evaluating DepGraph (CVPR 2023) and the open-source Torch-Pruning library to see where they work well and where they don't.

What the project involves

  • Checking which architectures can be pruned: CNNs, RNN/LSTM, Transformers and graph neural networks.
  • Building a PyTorch test harness that records whether pruning succeeds, crashes, or quietly gives wrong outputs.
  • Working towards pruning support for layers like LSTM/GRU, which I hope to contribute to Torch-Pruning.
  • Comparing methods on accuracy, model size and inference latency.

Supervisor: Sanka Mohottala · External supervisors: Mr. Asiri Gawesha (SLIIT), Dr. Dinuka Sahabandu (University of Washington)

In simple words

Big neural networks are often too slow for small devices like phones or a Jetson board. Structural pruning removes whole parts of a network (channels, neurons or attention heads) that contribute little, so the model becomes smaller and faster while trying to keep its accuracy.

The tricky part is that layers depend on each other: remove something in one layer and connected layers must change too. DepGraph tracks these dependencies automatically, and my project tests how reliably it does that across different kinds of networks.
